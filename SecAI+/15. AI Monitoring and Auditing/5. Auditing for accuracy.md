# Auditing for accuracy

- Assesses whether the AI system's outputs are correct and consistent for the tasks it performs
- Differs from auditing for hallucinations, which deals with fabricated information; inaccuracies can take the form of partial errors, misinterpretations, or incomplete answers
- The first step is to define clear metrics based on the system's function
  - A classification model audit may involve calculating the proportion of correct predictions out of all the tests performed
  - For a chatbot, accuracy could be measured by how often issues are resolved correctly as determined by human reviewers
  - In recommendation systems, accuracy may depend on how relevant the suggested items are to the user's needs
- The second step is to evaluate performance by testing against evaluation data
  - A gradual decline in accuracy on the same dataset may indicate concept drift, a condition which occurs when patterns in real-world data change, making the model's assumptions outdated
  - Retraining or recalibration may be needed to restore reliability
- Validate facts and sources to ensure that outputs are grounded in truth
  - Verify citations against credible evidence
- Ensure consistency and completeness
  - Correct answers must address the full scope of a user's request, not just part of it
- Incorporate user feedback
- Core auditing steps are:
  - Defining metrics
  - Factual validation
  - Systematic testing
  - User feedback
