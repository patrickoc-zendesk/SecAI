# Auditing for bias and fairness

- Aims to treat all individuals and groups equitably
- Bias occurs when an AI system produces unfair differences in outcomes among users
- Fairness means that decisions are consistent, just and free from discrimination
- Bias originates from 2 sources:
  - Data bias
    - Arises from training data which reflects social imbalance or stereotypes
  - Algorithmic bias
    - Results from the way models process data or assign weight to features
- Bias auditing steps:
  - Establish clear criteria
    - Use statistical measures such as demographic parity (do different groups receive similar results), equal opportunity (do equally qualified individuals have the same chance of success), and equalised odds (ensures that error rates remain consistent across groups) to help determine whether groups receive comparable outcomes
  - Test across subgroups
    - Test both data and model outputs across relevant subgroups to identify differences which may indicate fairness issues
  - Test across model types and modalities
  - Implement proactive fairness measures
    - Diverse and representative training data, apply fairness-aware algorithms, adjusting loss functions to encourage a balanced outcome
    - Continuous monitoring is required after deployment, recalibrating as needed to maintain fairness as data and conditions change over time
  - Incorporate diverse perspectives
  - Findings and mitigation steps should be documented for compliance with regulations such as the Equal Employment Opportunity Act or Fair Lending laws
