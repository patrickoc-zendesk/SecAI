# Model risk assessment

- The process of evaluating how an AI system performs under both normal and challenging conditions.
- Every model should be tested before deployment.
- This is done to ensure the model behaves safely, securely, and ethically.
- Especially important for LLMs that interact directly with users or make sensitive decisions.
- Security-focused evaluation looks at more than just accuracy.
  - It also examines how the model responds to manipulation, bias, and compliance requirements.
- During the assessment, testers deliberately try to uncover vulnerabilities before attackers can exploit them.
- Testers probe edge cases that push the model to its limits, ensuring it performs its core tasks reliably while adhering to established guidelines.
- Tools and frameworks are used to support model risk assessment:
  - Linux Foundation's Adversarial Robustness Toolbox (ART)
  - Microsoft's Counterfit
- These tools can be used to detect adversarial examples and data poisoning exposure.
- These help inform compensating controls and mitigation strategies.
- Model risk assessment is supported by the NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF):
  - Identify and document a model's capabilities, limitations, and risks, including its strengths and weaknesses, before deployment.
